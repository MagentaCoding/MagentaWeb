GDPR Violations at Tellter SAS / DoubleCounter / Doogle

News-style overview by Alexander Gal

Overview

Tellter SAS, a software and investment company based in Paris, France, is the parent company of DoubleCounter. This operates the platforms Doogle and Lens, which are used on Discord.

The platforms process personal data, including Discord IDs, activity data, and links to potential alternative accounts (“alts”). Initial investigations indicate that the processing systematically violates the GDPR.

Affected Users

All Discord users are potentially affected – even if they have never used Doogle or registered with DoubleCounter. Processing sometimes occurs without explicit consent or transparent information.

Platform Background

Doogle is a tool for analyzing Discord user IDs to identify potential alternative accounts. Lens records administrative actions such as bans, kicks, and mutes to document moderation processes. Both platforms are used by moderators to make community management more efficient.

According to internal documents (LIA & DPIA, May 2025), data processing is automated, encrypted, and supposed to be minimal. In practice, tests show that any user can use Doogle to search for other Discord IDs – regardless of whether these have a connection to DoubleCounter.

GDPR Violations in Detail

  • Article 5 – Principles of processing violated: data minimization, purpose limitation, storage limitation, and integrity not upheld.
  • Article 6 – No valid legal basis: processing occurs without consent.
  • Articles 12–14 – Information obligations not fulfilled: users are not sufficiently informed about processing.
  • Articles 15 & 17 – Access and deletion rights severely restricted; alt links remain even after opting out.
  • Article 21 – Objection to processing is limited, as opt-out works only via Discord server commands.
  • Article 25 – Data analysis occurs without user consent.
  • Article 32 – Access protection and data security insufficient; no complete imprint of Tellter SAS.
  • Articles 35 & 36 – Data protection impact assessment incomplete; risks for users insufficiently addressed.

Timeline of Events

May 2025: Publication of the Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA) and Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) for Doogle and Lens.
May 2025: Start of automated processing of Discord IDs and moderation metadata.
June 2025: Initial tests show that any Discord user can use Doogle to search for other IDs without their consent.
October 2025: Publication of an analysis by independent users (Alexander Gal) documenting the GDPR violations.

Consequences for Users

  • Links between accounts are created without consent.
  • Opt-out is limited; full protection not provided.
  • Affected users do not have full control over their data.
  • Processing occurs across borders – particularly between France and Germany.
  • Systemic risks for the entire Discord community.

Media Relevance and Public Awareness

The violations affect not only individual users but the entire Discord community. Media reports and investigative journalism could further clarify the legal situation, as opt-out and transparency are practically nonexistent. Reports to data protection authorities such as CNIL (France) and LDI (Germany) are already prepared.

Conclusion

Doogle, operated by DoubleCounter, part of Tellter SAS, shows systematic shortcomings in data protection. User rights are insufficiently protected, transparency is lacking, and existing opt-out options do not provide full protection. The violations are severe, affect the entire Discord community, and are relevant both legally and in the media.

Recommended Actions

  • Submit reports to CNIL (France) and LDI (Germany).
  • Document all violations, collect screenshots and test reports.
  • Public outreach via media and community channels to raise awareness.
  • Monitor responses from DoubleCounter / Doogle and enforce data subject rights.
